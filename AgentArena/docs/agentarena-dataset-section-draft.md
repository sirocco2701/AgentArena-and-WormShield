## IV. Dataset

We train WormShield on a synthetic but tightly controlled corpus generated inside AgentArena rather than on live operational traffic. This choice is necessary for both safety and scientific control. Real prompt-borne self-propagating attacks are difficult to obtain, difficult to label, and unsafe to collect in situ, whereas AgentArena provides a bounded environment in which every run can be reproduced, inspected, and terminated without exposing operational systems. The resulting corpus should therefore be interpreted as a research-grade approximation of future AI-native 6G control-plane communication, not as a replay of production traces.

Each scenario instantiates a communication graph of heterogeneous agents representing the types of intelligent entities expected in AI-native wireless systems, including orchestration nodes, slice-management controllers, assurance functions, edge relays, and security sentinels. These agents exchange telemetry summaries, policy updates, coordination messages, incident reports, and other control-oriented content over explicit topologies. Unlike datasets built from isolated prompts, AgentArena records how content is transformed, relayed, suppressed, escalated, quarantined, or absorbed as it moves through a distributed network-intelligence layer. This system-level view is important because an AI worm is defined not only by malicious content, but by its ability to remain semantically executable after passing through multiple relays.

To broaden coverage, we generate traces across four task families, five communication topologies, and five network sizes. The task families are RAN orchestration, slice management, assurance triage, and edge-mesh coordination. The topologies are chain, star, ring, mesh-lite, and random minimum-degree graphs, and the network sizes range from 4 to 20 agents. In the current deduped merged snapshot regenerated on July 30, 2026, these dimensions yield 836 accepted runs distributed across all task families, all five topologies, and all five graph sizes, with coverage ranging from 153 runs for edge-mesh coordination to 236 runs for RAN orchestration and from 147 runs for random minimum-degree topologies to 189 runs for chain topologies. This spread exposes WormShield to both shallow and deep relay paths, sparse and denser neighborhoods, and multiple operational contexts in which trust and forwarding behavior differ.

The corpus is also intentionally multi-model. The current combined batch pools the completed `paper-main-v1` campaign with the saved snapshot of `paper-main-v2`, using a deduped union by `runId` that prefers the later `paper-main-v2` record when both campaigns contain the same identifier. The resulting accepted runs contain 9,697 agent records drawn from local OpenAI-compatible runtimes (4,107 records), Azure OpenAI models (3,361 records), and Gemini models (2,229 records). This diversity is important because different runtime families exhibit different forwarding habits, refusal patterns, formatting artifacts, and susceptibility to indirect instruction following. By exposing WormShield to both local and cloud-hosted behaviors, the dataset reduces over-specialization to a single provider or model family and better reflects a mixed-vendor 6G deployment.

Each scenario is generated in matched malicious and benign forms. Malicious traces contain safe, non-operational abstractions of worm-like behavior that preserve observable propagation mechanics such as hidden-priority manipulation, guard suppression, recovery poisoning, and contamination of downstream summaries or shared context. Benign traces are designed as hard negatives rather than trivial clean examples; they include routine updates, maintenance notices, containment drills, and quoted suspicious content that should remain analyzable without being treated as executable instruction. This paired construction forces WormShield to learn propagation semantics and contextual intent rather than relying on keywords alone.

Labeling is derived from a simulator-side oracle based on observable message behavior rather than on model self-report. For each agent decision, AgentArena records the incoming lineage, forwarding action, outgoing text, downstream targets, warning indicators, and risk score. A malicious decision is labeled `propagating` only when two conditions hold simultaneously: the malicious payload remains present in the outgoing text, and the outgoing text still preserves the capability to relay that payload onward. If malicious context reaches a node but one of these properties is lost, the decision is labeled `exposed`. Benign non-malicious decisions are labeled `clean`, and runtime-fallback cases are labeled `uncertain`. This scheme matches the operational definition of an AI worm used in this work: the threat remains a worm only while both payload and relay capability survive each hop.

At the current deduped merged snapshot dated July 30, 2026, the accepted combined batch contains 836 runs, including 356 malicious and 480 benign scenarios. These runs yield 2,865 decision-level outputs and 2,174 forwarded inter-agent prompts. The decision labels are distributed as 1,312 `clean`, 504 `exposed`, 709 `propagating`, and 340 `uncertain`, with an average fallback rate of 0.0850 and an average of 1.31 malicious propagation messages per accepted run. These figures provide a dataset large enough to support message-level and run-level supervision while remaining interpretable at the scenario level.

All samples are subject to explicit safety and quality controls. AgentArena bounds hop depth, fan-out, turn count, and output budget, and rejects runs that exhibit unstable behavior, insufficient decision activity, inconsistent labels, or weak propagation evidence. These controls matter because the goal is not merely to maximize volume, but to curate traces that are informative, internally consistent, and useful for training a lightweight propagation-aware detector. The resulting corpus therefore serves as a reproducible training resource that connects 6G communication structure, multi-agent LLM behavior, and AI-worm defense in a form suitable for academic study.
