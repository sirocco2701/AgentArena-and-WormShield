Detecting and Containing AI-Worm

ABSTRACT Future 6G systems are expected to rely on increasingly distributed and autonomous AI
for network orchestration, management, and control, creating dense interactions among AI-enabled
components and new pathways for adversarial influence. In such environments, AI worms introduce a
distinct security challenge because malicious instructions can remain capable of propagation even after
being paraphrased, summarized, or transformed by large language models. Existing prompt-injection
defenses primarily identify malicious content or unsafe actions but do not directly determine whether
an interaction preserves the semantics required for continued propagation across agents. This paper
introduces AgentArena, a controlled multi-agent environment for injecting, observing, and tracing AI-
worm propagation across AI-native wireless systems, and WormShield, a relay-time defense designed to
detect and contain propagation before suspicious content reaches downstream agents. Using AgentArena,
we construct a propagation-oriented dataset capturing message transformation, forwarding behavior, and
propagation lineage across heterogeneous 6G-oriented tasks, communication topologies, network sizes,
and model runtimes. WormShield combines semantic representations of relay interactions with a learned
prompt-injection signal to distinguish active malicious propagation from benign communication and non-
propagatingmaliciousexposure.Onthe6G-orientedAgentArenadataset,WormShieldachievesanF1score
of 0.8562 ± 0.0109 and a false-positive rate of 0.0409 ± 0.0237, representing a 14.9 percentage-point F1
improvement over the strongest DonkeyRail baseline. On the external AI-Worm benchmark, WormShield
achieves an F1 score of 0.9878 ± 0.0020, showing that its effectiveness extends beyond the proposed 6G
environment. In offline counterfactual replay, WormShield reduces the mean attack reach from 22.31%
without defense to 0.70%, fully contains 95.78% of malicious runs, and limits benign false blocking
to 1.43%. These results demonstrate the value of controlled propagation analysis and propagation-aware
relay-time defense for improving the resilience of AI-native 6G systems.
